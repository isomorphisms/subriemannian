# Bibliography and acknowledgments

This is a working bibliography for sources actually used in the notes. It is not yet a transcription of every bibliography in every source. As reading proceeds, cited authors should be added by full public scholarly name, with the specific work and the place where it informed these notes.

## Primary spine

### Constantin Carathéodory

- Constantin Carathéodory, “Untersuchungen über die Grundlagen der Thermodynamik,” *Mathematische Annalen* **67** (1909), 355–386. DOI: [10.1007/BF01450409](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01450409).
- D. H. Delphenich, [English translation, “Examination of the Foundations of Thermodynamics”](https://neo-classical-physics.info/uploads/3/0/6/5/3065888/caratheodory_-_thermodynamics.pdf).
- Constantin Carathéodory, *Calculus of Variations and Partial Differential Equations of the First Order*, later English edition of his variational work.

### Richard Montgomery

- Richard Montgomery, *A Tour of Subriemannian Geometries, Their Geodesics and Applications*, Mathematical Surveys and Monographs 91, American Mathematical Society, 2002. [Publisher page](https://www.ams.org/books/surv/091/).
- Richard Montgomery, “A Survey of Singular Curves in Sub-Riemannian Geometry,” *Journal of Dynamical and Control Systems* **1** (1995), 49–90. DOI: [10.1007/BF02254656](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02254656). [Author-hosted PDF](https://peopleweb.prd.web.aws.ucsc.edu/~rmont/papers/surveySR.pdf).
- Richard Montgomery, “Abnormal Minimizers,” *SIAM Journal on Control and Optimization* **32** (1994), 1605–1620. DOI: [10.1137/S0363012993244945](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0363012993244945).
- Richard Montgomery, “Isoholonomic Problems and Some Applications,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* **128** (1990), 565–592.

### Enrico Le Donne

- Enrico Le Donne, [*Metric Lie Groups: Carnot–Carathéodory Spaces from the Homogeneous Viewpoint*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07291), arXiv:2410.07291. Published in Graduate Texts in Mathematics 306, Springer, 2025; online ISBN 978-3-031-98832-5.
- Enrico Le Donne, [*Lecture Notes on Sub-Riemannian Geometry: Carnot–Carathéodory Spaces from the Lie Group Viewpoint*](https://cvgmt.sns.it/media/doc/paper/5339/sub-Riem_notes%202023-5%20submitted_for_proposal.pdf), 2023 version.
- Enrico Le Donne, “A Primer on Carnot Groups: Homogenous Groups, Carnot–Carathéodory Spaces, and Regularity of Their Isometries,” *Analysis and Geometry in Metric Spaces* **5** (2017), 116–137. DOI: [10.1515/agms-2017-0007](https://doi.org/10.1515/agms-2017-0007).
- Enrico Le Donne, [sub-Riemannian geometry reading page](https://sites.google.com/view/enricoledonne/research/subriemannian-geometry).

## Foundational accessibility and distributions

- Wei-Liang Chow, “Über Systeme von linearen partiellen Differentialgleichungen erster Ordnung,” *Mathematische Annalen* **117** (1939), 98–105.
- Pyotr Konstantinovich Rashevskii, early work on connecting points by curves tangent to a nonintegrable distribution.
- Jacques Hadamard, work on Pfaffian equations and differential systems.
- Georges de Rham, work on differential forms and global topology.
- Charles Ehresmann, work on connections and horizontal distributions.
- Stefan-Sussmann orbit theorem: Peter Stefan and Héctor J. Sussmann.
- Robert Hermann, work connecting differential geometry, control, and constrained variational problems.

## Tangents, nilpotent approximation, and metric geometry

- John Mitchell, “On Carnot–Carathéodory Metrics,” *Journal of Differential Geometry* **21** (1985), 35–45.
- André Bellaïche, “The Tangent Space in Sub-Riemannian Geometry,” in André Bellaïche and Jean-Jacques Risler, editors, *Sub-Riemannian Geometry*, Progress in Mathematics 144, Birkhäuser, 1996, 1–78.
- Mikhael Gromov, “Carnot–Carathéodory Spaces Seen from Within,” in *Sub-Riemannian Geometry*, Progress in Mathematics 144, Birkhäuser, 1996, 79–323.
- Pierre Pansu, “Métriques de Carnot–Carathéodory et quasiisométries des espaces symétriques de rang un,” *Annals of Mathematics* **129** (1989), 1–60.
- Grigory Margulis and George Mostow, work on differentiability and tangent cones in Carnot–Carathéodory spaces.
- Valerii Berestovskii, work characterizing intrinsic metrics on homogeneous manifolds.
- Andrew Gleason, Deane Montgomery, and Leo Zippin, work underlying the solution of Hilbert’s fifth problem and the Lie structure of transformation groups.
- Dmitri Burago, Yuri Burago, and Sergei Ivanov, *A Course in Metric Geometry*.
- Juha Heinonen, *Lectures on Analysis on Metric Spaces*.

## Hamiltonian and control viewpoints

- Andrei Agrachev, Davide Barilari, and Ugo Boscain, *A Comprehensive Introduction to Sub-Riemannian Geometry: From the Hamiltonian Viewpoint*, Cambridge University Press, 2020. [Open manuscript](https://people.sissa.it/~agrachev/agrachev_files/ABB-final-SRnotes.pdf).
- Lev Pontryagin, Vladimir Boltyanskii, Revaz Gamkrelidze, and Evgenii Mishchenko, *The Mathematical Theory of Optimal Processes*.
- Héctor J. Sussmann, work on geometric control, singular trajectories, and orbit theorems.
- Wensheng Liu and Héctor J. Sussmann, *Shortest Paths for Sub-Riemannian Metrics on Rank-Two Distributions*, Memoirs of the American Mathematical Society 118, no. 564, 1995.
- Jean-Michel Bismut, work on hypoelliptic operators, stochastic analysis, and control-geometric path spaces.
- Frédéric Jean, *Control of Nonholonomic Systems: From Sub-Riemannian Geometry to Motion Planning*.
- Ludovic Rifford, *Sub-Riemannian Geometry and Optimal Transport*.

## Singular and abnormal curves

- Robert Bryant and Lucas Hsu, work on rigidity of integral curves of rank-two distributions.
- Jean Martinet, “Sur les singularités des formes différentielles,” *Annales de l’Institut Fourier* **20** (1970), 95–178.
- Ivan Kupka, work on abnormal extremals and singularities of control systems.
- Andrei Agrachev and Andrey Sarychev, work on abnormal geodesics, Morse index, and rigidity.
- Roberto Monti, work on regularity of sub-Riemannian geodesics.
- Valentino Magnani, Davide Vittone, Gian Paolo Leonardi, and collaborators, work on extremal curves and geometric measure theory in Carnot groups.

## Analysis on Carnot groups

- Lars Hörmander, work on hypoelliptic differential operators and bracket conditions.
- Linda Rothschild and Elias Stein, “Hypoelliptic Differential Operators and Nilpotent Groups,” *Acta Mathematica* **137** (1976), 247–320.
- Gerald Folland and Elias Stein, *Hardy Spaces on Homogeneous Groups*.
- Bruno Franchi, Raul Serapioni, and Francesco Serra Cassano, work on rectifiability and finite-perimeter sets in Heisenberg and Carnot groups.
- Luigi Ambrosio, Bruce Kleiner, and Enrico Le Donne, work on tangents of finite-perimeter sets in Carnot groups.
- Jeff Cheeger and Bruce Kleiner, work on metric differentiation and mappings into $L^1$.

## Thermodynamic foundations around Carathéodory

- Josiah Willard Gibbs, especially *On the Equilibrium of Heterogeneous Substances*.
- Sadi Carnot, Rudolf Clausius, William Thomson (Lord Kelvin), Julius Robert von Mayer, and James Prescott Joule, whose work forms the physical background to Carathéodory’s axiomatization.
- Max Planck, for both thermodynamic foundations and the contemporary debate over Carathéodory’s formulation.
- L. Pogliani and M. N. Berberan-Santos, “Constantin Carathéodory and the Axiomatic Thermodynamics,” *Journal of Mathematical Chemistry* **28** (2000), 313–324.
- H. A. Buchdahl, for later formulations and proofs of Carathéodory’s theorem.
- Elliott Lieb and Jakob Yngvason, for entropy defined through adiabatic accessibility and order.
- Theodore Frankel, for geometric presentations of differential forms, constraints, and thermodynamics.

## Explicit thanks

Thanks to Constantin Carathéodory for making local accessibility a mathematical foundation problem; to D. H. Delphenich for making the 1909 paper readily available in English; to Richard Montgomery for the concrete route from Dido and Heisenberg to abnormal extremals; and to Enrico Le Donne for organizing Carnot–Carathéodory geometry around metric Lie groups, tangents, and homogeneous limits.

Thanks also to Wei-Liang Chow and Pyotr Rashevskii for the accessibility theorem; to John Mitchell, André Bellaïche, Mikhael Gromov, and Pierre Pansu for the modern tangent, metric, and differentiation theory; to Andrei Agrachev, Davide Barilari, Ugo Boscain, Héctor J. Sussmann, Wensheng Liu, Robert Bryant, Lucas Hsu, Jean Martinet, and Richard Montgomery for the theory of geodesics and singular curves; and to all authors listed above whose definitions, examples, proofs, bibliographies, or historical work support these notes.

A later bibliography pass should expand acknowledgments source by source rather than use “et al.” or silently absorb a bibliography. That pass should distinguish:

1. works actually read;
2. works cited by a source but not yet inspected;
3. historical attributions that need verification;
4. translators, editors, and maintainers who made texts accessible.
